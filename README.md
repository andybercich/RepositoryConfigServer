**# Microservices Business Management \& Analytics Platform**



**## Overview**



**This project is a backend system designed for small and medium-sized businesses that need to manage orders, products, inventory, shipments, and business analytics through a microservices-based architecture.**



**In addition to the core management functionality, the platform includes an analytics subsystem capable of processing sales and inventory events and generating monthly business reports. It also integrates a Groq-powered AI agent that analyzes business data and provides recommendations based on the information generated by the system.**



**The main objective is to centralize operational management while reducing the need for business owners to manually analyze large amounts of sales, inventory, and reporting data.**



**## Problem**



**Managing orders, products, inventory, and shipments often requires information to be distributed across different systems or manually analyzed.**



**This project addresses this problem by separating business responsibilities into independent services while using event-driven communication to build an analytical view of the business.**



**The Analytics Service processes information generated by the order and product domains and produces metrics such as revenue, profit, margins, sales performance, product performance, and inventory movements.**



**Monthly reports provide a consolidated view of this information, while the AI integration adds an additional layer of interpretation and recommendations.**



**## Architecture**



**The system follows a microservices architecture combining synchronous REST communication with asynchronous event-driven communication through Apache Kafka.**



**### Main Components**



**- \*\*API Gateway\*\* — Entry point for requests that are routed through the gateway. It also participates in authentication and authorization.**

**- \*\*Eureka Server\*\* — Service discovery and registration.**

**- \*\*Auth Service\*\* — User management and JWT authentication.**

**- \*\*Pedido Service\*\* — Order management, stock-related operations, shipment tracking, and order event publishing.**

**- \*\*Product Service\*\* — Product, category, pack, and inventory management, including stock movement events.**

**- \*\*Analytics Service\*\* — Event consumption, analytics persistence, monthly reporting, charts, and AI-based business analysis.**

**- \*\*Apache Kafka\*\* — Asynchronous communication between business services.**

**- \*\*Zookeeper\*\* — Kafka coordination in the current local setup.**

**- \*\*Common Events\*\* — Shared library containing the Kafka event contracts used across services.**

**- \*\*N8N\*\* — Automation service used for shipment-status email notifications.**

**- \*\*FedEx API\*\* — External shipment tracking integration used by the Pedido Service.**

**- \*\*Groq API\*\* — OpenAI-compatible API used by the Analytics Service for AI-powered business analysis.**

**## Microservices**



**### Auth Service**



**Responsible for user management and authentication.**



**Main responsibilities:**



**- User creation and persistence.**

**- Authentication.**

**- Password hashing using BCrypt.**

**- JWT generation.**

**- Role and authority management.**

**- Supporting `ADMIN` and `USER` roles.**



**The `ADMIN` role represents the business owner or administrator, while `USER` is intended for employees who perform operational tasks such as order and inventory management.**



**### Pedido Service**



**Responsible for the order lifecycle.**



**Main responsibilities include:**



**- Creating and managing orders.**

**- Obtaining product prices and costs from the Product Service.**

**- Applying inventory-related operations.**

**- Publishing order events.**

**- Managing shipment information.**

**- Periodically querying the FedEx API to update shipment status.**



**The service uses the Transactional Outbox Pattern to improve reliability when publishing Kafka events.**



**### Product Service**



**Responsible for product and inventory management.**



**Main responsibilities include:**



**- Product management.**

**- Category management.**

**- Pack management.**

**- Stock management.**

**- Stock movement registration.**

**- Publishing inventory-related events to Kafka.**



**### Analytics Service**



**Responsible for collecting and processing business events generated by the operational services.**



**It consumes order and stock movement events and maintains its own analytical data model.**



**The service provides:**



**- Sales analytics.**

**- Profit and margin analysis.**

**- Product performance analysis.**

**- Inventory movement analysis.**

**- Monthly analytics.**

**- PDF reports.**

**- Charts.**

**- AI-powered business analysis and recommendations.**



**### API Gateway**



**Provides centralized routing for requests that enter through the gateway.**



**It also participates in:**



**- JWT validation.**

**- Authorization.**

**- Request routing.**

**- Communication with services registered through Eureka.**



**### Eureka Server**



**Provides service discovery so that microservices can locate each other without relying exclusively on hard-coded service addresses.**

**## Kafka Event Architecture**



**Kafka is used for asynchronous communication between the business services and the Analytics Service.**



**Only the Pedido Service and Product Service act as event producers.**



**The Analytics Service consumes the events generated by both services.**



**### Topics**



**The current Kafka topics are:**



**```text**

**order-created-events**

**order-delete-events**

**order-updated-events**

**stock-movement-events**

**```**

**## Reliability and Event Publishing**



**The Pedido Service and Product Service use the Transactional Outbox Pattern.**



**Instead of directly depending on Kafka availability during the business transaction, the service stores the business operation and its corresponding event in the database within the same transaction.**



**The event is later published by an independent publishing process.**



**```text**

**Business Operation**

&#x20;      **│**

&#x20;      **▼**

**┌──────────────────────┐**

**│ Database Transaction │**

**│                      │**

**│ Business Data        │**

**│ +                    │**

**│ Outbox Event         │**

**└──────────┬───────────┘**

&#x20;          **│**

&#x20;          **▼**

&#x20;     **Outbox Scheduler**

&#x20;          **│**

&#x20;          **▼**

&#x20;        **Kafka**

**```**



**If Kafka is temporarily unavailable, the event remains pending in the Outbox instead of being lost.**



**Pending events are retried by a scheduler every 30 minutes. The system also records the last publication attempt and whether that attempt succeeded.**



**A Dead Letter Queue is not currently implemented in the deployment.**



**## Idempotency**



**The Analytics Service implements event idempotency to prevent the same event from generating duplicate analytical records.**



**Processed events are persisted using their event identifier.**



**```text**

**Kafka Event**

&#x20;    **│**

&#x20;    **▼**

**Check eventId**

&#x20;    **│**

&#x20;**┌───┴───────────┐**

&#x20;**│               │**

**Exists          New**

&#x20;**│               │**

&#x20;**▼               ▼**

**Ignore       Process Event**

&#x20;                **│**

&#x20;                **▼**

&#x20;         **Store ProcessedEvent**

&#x20;                **│**

&#x20;                **▼**

&#x20;         **Store Analytics Data**

**```**



**This is particularly important in an event-driven system where the same event may potentially be delivered more than once.**



**## Security**



**The system uses JWT-based authentication and authorization.**



**There are two roles:**



**```text**

**ADMIN**

**USER**

**```**



**Passwords are stored using BCrypt rather than plain text.**



**JWT tokens contain the user's roles/authorities and are used to authorize protected operations.**



**When a request enters through the API Gateway, the Gateway validates the token and the required authorization before forwarding the request to the corresponding service.**



**There are no intentionally public business endpoints.**



**The intended access model is:**



**```text**

**ADMIN**

&#x20;**├── Manage products**

&#x20;**├── Access business analytics**

&#x20;**└── Generate/access reports**



**USER**

&#x20;**├── Manage orders**

&#x20;**└── Perform operational inventory tasks**

**```**



**## Analytics**



**The Analytics Service builds an analytical representation of the operational data without requiring the business services themselves to perform complex reporting queries.**



**Order events provide information about sales and profitability.**



**Product events provide information about inventory movements.**



**The resulting analytical data can be used to calculate metrics such as:**



**- Revenue.**

**- Profit.**

**- Profit margin.**

**- Average order value.**

**- Number of orders.**

**- Number of products/items sold.**

**- Top-performing products.**

**- Best sales days.**

**- Best revenue days.**

**- Revenue by week.**

**- Profit by week.**

**- Stock movements.**

**- Products with the highest losses.**

**- Products generating the highest profit.**

**- Total units lost.**



**Monthly analytics are persisted by year and month, allowing the system to generate consolidated business reports.**



**## Monthly Reports**



**The Analytics Service generates monthly business reports in PDF format.**



**Reports consolidate sales, profitability, product, and inventory information into a single document.**



**Charts are generated as part of the reporting process to provide a visual representation of relevant business metrics.**



**The reporting subsystem uses:**



**- \*\*iText\*\* for PDF generation.**

**- \*\*JFreeChart\*\* for chart generation.**



**An example report-generation endpoint is:**



**```http**

**GET /reports/generate/monthly?year={year}\&month={month}**

**```**



**The exact available endpoints and parameters are documented in each service's README.**



**## AI Integration**



**The Analytics Service integrates with the Groq API through an OpenAI-compatible API.**



**The AI layer does not act as the source of business data.**



**Instead, the Analytics Service calculates and provides the relevant business information, which is then interpreted by the AI.**



**The purpose of the AI integration is to help business owners understand the generated analytics without requiring them to manually analyze every metric or graph.**



**The AI can provide:**



**- Business summaries.**

**- Sales and profit analysis.**

**- Product concentration analysis.**

**- Loss analysis.**

**- Purchase recommendations.**

**- Organization recommendations.**

**- Interpretation of business graphs and metrics.**



**The AI is expected to base its conclusions only on the information provided by the Analytics Service and should not invent business data.**

**## Architectural Decisions**



**Several architectural decisions were made to keep business responsibilities separated and make the system more resilient.**



**### Microservices**



**Business domains are separated into independent services so that order management, product/inventory management, authentication, and analytics have independent responsibilities.**



**### Event-Driven Analytics**



**Analytics does not need to execute complex queries directly against the operational services.**



**Instead, it consumes domain events and maintains its own analytical representation.**



**### Transactional Outbox**



**The Outbox Pattern prevents business events from being lost when Kafka is temporarily unavailable.**



**### Shared Event Contracts**



**The `common-events` module provides shared Kafka event definitions used by producers and consumers.**



**### Idempotent Event Processing**



**The Analytics Service tracks processed event IDs to avoid duplicate analytical records.**



**### JWT Authentication**



**Authentication and authorization are handled through stateless JWT tokens with role-based access control.**



**### AI as an Interpretation Layer**



**The AI does not replace the application's business logic or database.**



**The application remains responsible for calculating and providing real business data, while the AI focuses on interpretation and recommendations.**
